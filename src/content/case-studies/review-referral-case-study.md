We run an ongoing review and referral system that consistently captures 10 to 20% of a business's past customer list into new 5-star reviews and referrals.

## How it works

**Controlled daily outreach.** We drip out SMS and email requests between 15 and 25 per day, depending on the size of the client's past customer list. This keeps it from looking like a mass blast and keeps response quality high.

**Personalized messaging.** Each message includes an image with the customer's name and a photo of the actual technician, crew, or sales rep who worked with them. People respond to a real face and a personal touch a lot better than a generic company logo.

**A filtering survey before the review request.** Before anyone is sent to leave a public review, we ask how their experience was.
- *Bad experience:* it never goes public. It routes straight to the manager, and we automatically create a task so someone calls that customer to make it right.
- *Good experience:* they're sent directly to the review platform (Google, primarily) to leave feedback while the experience is fresh.

**Referral and loyalty follow-up.** After a happy customer leaves a review, they're automatically moved into a referral and loyalty sequence. We typically set up a double-sided referral program — both sides win. For example: refer a friend, get $300 when they become a customer, and your friend gets $300 off their first service too.

**Ongoing automation, not just a one-time push.** Once this is set up, every new appointment or estimate automatically triggers the review and referral sequence going forward, so the business keeps building reviews and referrals on autopilot instead of relying on someone remembering to ask.

**NFC, QR codes & team training.** We implement physical NFC tap cards and QR code displays to capture reviews instantly on the job site. Plus, we provide your team with proven scripting and a mini-training on exactly when and how to ask customers for reviews, making it a natural part of the service rather than an awkward request.
