A business reached out to us confused and frustrated. They weren't getting calls from Google, their phone had gone quiet, and they had no idea why. On paper, they had a website. It existed. It looked fine. But when we ran a full audit, we found the real problem: not a single page on their site was indexed by Google or Bing.

## Why this happens (and why DIY website builders miss it)

This is one of the most common and most damaging mistakes we see, and it almost always traces back to the same root cause: a DIY website builder or a previous developer who built something that looked like a website but wasn't actually built to be crawled and indexed correctly.

Here's the thing most business owners don't know: having a website and having a website Google can actually find, read, and rank are two completely different things. A site can be live, look professional, and still be sitting in the exact same spot with zero visibility.

> Organic search accounts for roughly 53% of all website traffic, but that number only applies to pages Google can actually see. If your pages aren't indexed, that 53% simply doesn't exist for you. You're not underperforming in search, you're absent from it entirely.

## What we found in the audit

- Zero indexed pages across both Google and Bing
- No functioning sitemap being read correctly
- Search Console showing no discovered pages, meaning Google wasn't even attempting to crawl the site properly

## What we did

- **Redesigned the website from the ground up**, built specifically to be crawlable and indexable, not just visually presentable.
- **Submitted a proper, working sitemap** so Google could actually discover every page instead of guessing at the site structure.
- **Fixed the underlying technical issues** blocking indexation in the first place, the kind of thing a DIY builder or template site often gets wrong without anyone knowing until it's too late.
- **Verified indexing through Search Console** to confirm Google was actually crawling and storing the pages, not just attempting to.

## The results

| Metric | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sitemap status | Not functioning | Success |
| Discovered pages | 0 | 45 |
| Indexed by Google/Bing | No | Yes |

Once the sitemap was corrected and resubmitted, Google immediately discovered 45 pages that had effectively not existed to search before. Every one of those pages is now a potential entry point for a new customer to find this business.

## DIY vs. professional: what's actually at stake

| Feature | DIY / template website | Professionally built & optimized |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Looks good | Often yes | Yes |
| Actually indexable by Google | Not guaranteed | Verified and confirmed |
| Sitemap functions correctly | Frequently broken or missing | Built and tested |
| Ongoing technical monitoring | None | Ongoing |
| Risk of being invisible to search | High and often undetected | Actively prevented |

The scary part of this client's situation wasn't that their rankings were bad. It's that nobody would have ever told them the problem existed. A DIY site can run for years looking completely normal to the owner while being completely invisible to the customers searching for exactly what they offer.

## Why this matters

This business didn't need a bigger marketing budget or more ad spend. They needed their existing website to actually function the way a website is supposed to function — discoverable, crawlable, and indexed. Once that was fixed, the traffic and calls they were expecting to see from Google finally had a path to actually reach them.

If you built your site yourself or through a cheap template service and you're not sure whether Google can even see it, that's worth checking before spending another dollar on ads or marketing to drive people to a site that search engines can't find.
