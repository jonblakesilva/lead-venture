Contractors ask us this constantly: "Should I just get ServiceTitan?" The honest answer is that ServiceTitan and a HighLevel-based platform aren't really competing for the same job. Comparing them head-to-head is a little like comparing a dispatch office to a marketing department — both matter, but they solve different problems.

## What ServiceTitan actually is

ServiceTitan is field-service management (FSM) software: dispatch boards, job costing, technician GPS tracking, inventory, payroll integration, and deep reporting. It's built for businesses that already have volume — multiple crews, an office team coordinating them, and enough revenue to justify the investment. Publicly reported pricing runs roughly $245–$400+ per technician per month, plus a $5,000–$50,000 implementation fee and a mandatory annual contract, so a 10-tech shop can be looking at $35,000–$65,000 in year one. (ServiceTitan doesn't publish pricing directly — these are reported ranges from resellers and pricing-analysis sites, not a rate card.)

The reviews are consistent: it's the most feature-complete platform on the market if you need real dispatch and inventory management, and the onboarding is genuinely hard — a 3-6 month ramp isn't unusual, and smaller accounts report inconsistent support. Most reviewers agree it's the wrong tool under roughly 15 techs or $5M in revenue. Below that, you're paying enterprise prices for features you won't use yet.

## What a HighLevel-based platform actually is

GoHighLevel isn't field-service software at all — it has no dispatch board, no technician job workflows, no parts or inventory tracking. What it does well is the front end: missed-call text-back, two-way SMS follow-up, automated review requests, a funnel and website builder, and pipeline tracking for leads. Pricing is flat and predictable — commonly $97 to $497 a month depending on tier — with no per-tech cost and no long-term contract lock-in.

## The actual decision

If the problem is "we're not getting enough leads, or we're losing the ones we get," ServiceTitan doesn't fix that — it's not built to. If the problem is "we have more leads than our office can coordinate, and jobs are falling through operational cracks," a HighLevel-based platform won't fix that either — it's not built to schedule technicians or track parts.

For most shops under $5M in revenue, the honest advice is: get the marketing and CRM side solid first — that's the cheaper problem to fix and it's usually the bigger lever on revenue anyway. Once you're running enough volume that dispatch and inventory are the actual bottleneck, an FSM platform earns its cost. Plenty of $1M-$10M shops run both — a HighLevel-based system on the front end for lead capture and follow-up, a dedicated FSM for job execution once volume justifies it.
