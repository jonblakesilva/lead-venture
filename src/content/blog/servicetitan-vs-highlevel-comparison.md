Contractors ask us this all the time: "Should I just get ServiceTitan?"

The honest answer is that ServiceTitan and a HighLevel-based platform aren't really trying to do the same job. Comparing them side by side is a bit like comparing a dispatch office to a marketing department. Both matter. They just solve completely different problems.

This post breaks down exactly what each one does, what they actually cost, and how to know which one you need first.

## What ServiceTitan actually is

ServiceTitan is **field-service management software**, often called FSM for short. That means it's built to run the day-to-day operations of jobs you've already booked:

- A dispatch board showing which tech is where
- Technician GPS tracking
- Deep inventory and parts tracking
- Payroll integration
- Detailed reporting on every job and every tech

It's built for businesses that already have real volume: multiple crews, an office team coordinating all of them, and enough revenue to justify a big investment.

### What it actually costs

Publicly reported pricing runs roughly **$245 to $400+ per technician, per month**, plus an implementation fee of **$5,000 to $50,000**, plus a required annual contract. For a shop with 10 techs, that can add up to **$35,000 to $65,000 in the first year alone**. (ServiceTitan doesn't publish an official price list — these figures come from resellers and independent pricing write-ups, so always get a real quote before deciding.)

### What real users say

The reviews are fairly consistent on two points. First, it's the most complete platform on the market if you genuinely need real dispatch and inventory management at scale. Second, the onboarding is hard — a 3 to 6 month ramp-up isn't unusual, even for teams that know what they're doing, and smaller accounts sometimes report slower support.

Most people who've actually used it agree on one thing: below roughly **15 technicians or $5 million in revenue**, it's usually the wrong tool. You end up paying enterprise prices for features your business isn't big enough to use yet.

## What a HighLevel-based platform actually is

GoHighLevel is a completely different kind of tool. It has **no dispatch board, no technician job workflows, and no parts or inventory tracking.** It's not trying to run your jobs at all.

What it's actually built for is the front end of your business — everything that happens *before* a job gets scheduled:

- **Missed-call text-back**, so a call you couldn't answer still gets an instant response
- **Two-way SMS follow-up**, so a lead who doesn't book right away gets nudged automatically instead of forgotten
- **Automated review requests**, sent out after a job is marked complete
- **A funnel and website builder**, for building landing pages without hiring a developer
- **Pipeline tracking**, so you can see exactly where every lead is in your sales process

### What it actually costs

Pricing is flat and predictable — commonly **$97 to $497 a month**, depending on the plan. There's no per-technician charge, and no long-term contract locking you in.

## The side-by-side comparison

| | ServiceTitan | GoHighLevel-based platform |
|---|---|---|
| **What it's for** | Running jobs you've already booked | Capturing and following up on leads before they're booked |
| **Has a dispatch board?** | Yes | No |
| **Tracks parts and inventory?** | Yes | No |
| **Typical monthly cost** | $245–$400+ per tech | $97–$497 flat, no per-tech cost |
| **Setup time** | 3–6 months | Days to a few weeks |
| **Contract** | Usually a required annual contract | No long-term lock-in |
| **Best fit** | 15+ techs or $5M+ revenue | Any size, especially under $5M |

## The actual decision you need to make

Ask yourself which sentence describes your real problem right now:

**"We're not getting enough leads, or we're losing the ones we get."** If this is you, ServiceTitan doesn't fix that — it was never built to. It manages jobs that already exist; it doesn't generate or rescue leads.

**"We have more leads than our office can coordinate, and jobs are falling through the cracks."** If this is you, a HighLevel-based platform won't fix it either — it has no dispatch board and can't schedule your technicians or track your parts.

For most shops under $5 million in revenue, the honest advice is: **fix the marketing and follow-up side first.** It's the cheaper problem to solve, and it's usually the bigger lever on revenue anyway — a lead you never lose is worth more than a job you run slightly more efficiently. Once you're running enough volume that dispatch and inventory genuinely become your bottleneck, a full FSM platform starts to earn its cost.

Plenty of businesses between $1 million and $10 million in revenue run both at once: a HighLevel-based system on the front end for lead capture and follow-up, and a dedicated FSM platform for running jobs once volume actually justifies it.

## A quick gut-check before you spend a dollar

Answer these honestly:

1. Do leads sometimes go a full day or more without a follow-up? → Fix the front end first.
2. Are jobs getting double-booked, or is a tech showing up without the right parts? → You may be ready for an FSM platform.
3. Do you have fewer than 15 techs? → An FSM platform's price tag probably won't pay for itself yet.
4. Is your current CRM (if you have one at all) a truck-count-appropriate tool from our [CRM comparison guide](/blog/best-crm-for-plumbers)? → Start there before adding anything new.
