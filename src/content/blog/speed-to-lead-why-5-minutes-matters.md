Most contractors think they lose jobs on price. In reality, they lose most of them on speed.

When a homeowner has a leaking pipe, a dead AC unit, or a tree branch through their fence, they don't call one company. They call three or four, and they hire whoever answers first with a real plan. If that's not you, it doesn't matter how good your work is — you never got the chance to prove it.

This post covers what "speed to lead" means, why it matters more than almost anything else in your marketing, and the exact system to fix it.

## What does "speed to lead" mean?

**Speed to lead** is simply how fast you respond after someone reaches out to you — whether that's a phone call, a missed call, or a form filled out on your website. It's measured in minutes, sometimes seconds.

That's the whole definition. It sounds simple. Most businesses still get it wrong.

## What the data says about response time

Studies on inbound lead response, across many different industries, all point to the same pattern: **leads contacted within 5 minutes are dramatically more likely to become paying customers than leads contacted even 30 minutes later.**

Once you cross that 30-minute mark, conversion rates drop sharply. By that point, most homeowners have already booked someone else — often the first company that called them back, not the best one.

Here's the part that should sting: **most local service businesses don't respond within 5 minutes.** Not because they don't care about their customers. It's because they're on a ladder, under a sink, or driving between job sites the exact moment the phone rings. The call goes to voicemail. The homeowner moves to the next name on their list. You never even know you lost the job.

## Why missed calls are the real leak — not weak marketing

A lot of contractors spend money trying to generate more leads before ever fixing what happens to the leads they already have. That's backwards, and it's an expensive mistake.

Here's the math: if you're missing even one in five calls, and nobody follows up fast on those missed calls, you are literally paying to generate leads that disappear before your team ever talks to them. More ad spend won't fix that — it just means you lose more leads, faster.

## The fix: three things, not one big software purchase

The fix isn't "answer every single call." That's not realistic when you're running a crew and your hands are covered in grease at 2pm. The real fix is making sure a missed call never goes completely silent.

### 1. Automatic text-back on every missed call

The instant you miss a call, the caller gets a text. Something simple, like:

> "Hi, this is [Your Business Name] — sorry we missed your call! We saw you reached out and we'll call you back shortly. If it's urgent, reply here and let us know what's going on."

This does two things: it tells the caller you actually saw their call (so they don't assume you're ignoring them), and it gives them a way to reply by text if calling back isn't convenient for them right now.

### 2. Instant follow-up on web form submissions

The same principle applies to anyone who fills out a form on your website. An automated first-touch response — sent within seconds, not hours — keeps that lead warm while you finish the job you're on.

### 3. A simple internal alert to a real human

Whoever owns sales at your company should get notified the moment a new lead comes in — a text, an app notification, something immediate. That way a real person can jump into the conversation while it's still hot, instead of finding out about the lead at the end of the day.

## A simple callback script that works

Once you do call back — even if it's 20 minutes later instead of 5 — how you open the call matters. Try this:

> "Hi, this is [Your Name] from [Business Name], returning your call about [the problem, if you know it]. Sorry for the short delay — I was on another job site. What's going on, and how can I help?"

Notice this script does three things: it identifies you clearly, it apologizes briefly without over-explaining, and it gets straight into helping them. No sales pitch, no filler — just showing up and being useful fast.

## A simple self-test: how fast are you actually responding?

Try this over the next week. It costs nothing and takes five minutes to set up:

1. Have a friend or family member call your business number and hang up before you answer (a missed call).
2. Time how long it takes for any response — text or call — to reach them.
3. Do the same test with a form submission on your website.
4. Write down both numbers.

If either number is longer than 5 minutes, you likely have a real, fixable leak — and now you know exactly how big it is.

## What actually changes when you fix this

Businesses that fix speed to lead don't necessarily spend a cent more on ads. They just stop losing the leads they're already paying for. That's usually the fastest, cheapest win available to a local service business — faster than a new website, faster than a rebrand, faster than chasing some new marketing channel you haven't tried yet.
