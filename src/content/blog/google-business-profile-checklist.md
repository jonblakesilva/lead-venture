Almost every contractor has a Google Business Profile. Very few have an optimized one.

There's a real difference between "claimed" and "optimized," and that gap is usually the reason a competitor with worse work shows up above you in the map pack. Google rewards profiles that look active, complete, and trustworthy — and most businesses set theirs up once, years ago, and never touch it again.

## What "optimized" actually means

Run through this list honestly. Most businesses are missing at least half of it.

- **Every category filled in, not just the primary one.** Google lets you add secondary categories — use them. A roofer that also does gutters and siding should be listed for all three.
- **Real photos, updated regularly.** Job-site photos, before/afters, your crew, your trucks. Stock photos and a five-year-old logo don't help you rank and don't build trust with someone scrolling on their phone.
- **Services listed individually**, not lumped into one vague description. "Roof repair," "roof replacement," and "storm damage inspection" are three separate searches — make sure you show up for all of them.
- **Posts published on a regular cadence.** Google Posts are an underused feature that signals an active, maintained profile. A seasonal promotion or a recent job photo posted monthly does more than people expect.
- **Q&A section seeded and monitored.** Anyone can ask (and answer) questions on your profile. If you haven't seeded the obvious ones yourself, someone else — possibly a competitor — might answer them for you.
- **Reviews responded to, good and bad.** A profile with 40 reviews and zero owner responses looks abandoned. Responding to every review, especially the negative ones, is one of the strongest trust signals you can send.

## Why this matters more than a new website

A homeowner searching "roofer near me" almost never scrolls past the map pack. If your profile isn't in the top three results, your beautiful new website doesn't matter — they'll never see it. Google Business Profile optimization is often the highest-leverage, lowest-cost thing a local service business can do, because it directly controls whether you show up in the exact moment someone is ready to call.

This is also why review velocity matters so much: businesses that consistently generate new reviews tend to outrank businesses with more total reviews but no recent activity. Freshness counts.
