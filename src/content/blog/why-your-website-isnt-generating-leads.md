We talk to a lot of contractors who assume their website's problem is a design problem. It usually isn't.

Most of the sites we review look perfectly fine. Clean layout, decent photos, the logo sits where it should. And they still generate almost no leads. That's because a website can look completely fine and still fail at the one job it actually has: turning a visitor into a phone call or a form fill before they click away.

This post explains the real difference between a website that just sits there and one that actually brings in work — plus a checklist you can run on your own site right now.

## Brochure site vs. lead-gen site: what's the actual difference?

A **brochure site** describes your business. It has a home page, an about page, maybe a services page. It reads like a printed pamphlet that happens to live online.

A **lead-gen site** is built around one single question, answered clearly on every single page: **what does this visitor do next?**

That sounds obvious once you say it out loud. But most contractor websites never actually answer it. Here are the five most common gaps we see, over and over.

## Gap #1: No clear call to action above the fold

"Above the fold" just means the part of the page you see without scrolling. If a homeowner has to scroll down to find your phone number or a "Request a Quote" button, most of them simply won't bother. They'll hit the back button and call the next name on their list instead.

**Fix:** Your phone number and a clear, visible action button should both be visible the instant the page loads — on desktop and on mobile.

## Gap #2: Lead forms that ask for too much, too early

A five-field form with a dropdown menu for "budget range" feels like signing a contract, not sending a quick message. Most visitors will simply give up halfway through.

**Fix:** Ask for a name, a phone number, and one short line describing the problem. That's it. You can always ask follow-up questions on the phone once you have them on the line. A short form that actually gets submitted beats a detailed form that gets abandoned.

## Gap #3: Slow load times on mobile

Most local service searches happen on a phone — often while someone is standing right in front of the exact problem they need fixed. If your site takes six seconds to load, you lose a big chunk of visitors before they even see your headline.

**Fix:** Test your site with Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool. Compress large images, and avoid heavy background videos on your homepage if they're slowing things down.

## Gap #4: No proof near the decision point

Reviews, before-and-after photos, and licensing or insurance badges work best when they sit right next to your call-to-action button — not buried on a separate "About Us" page that almost nobody clicks into.

**Fix:** Put a short review or two, plus your license number and insurance badge, directly next to (or just above) every "Request a Quote" button on the page. The proof should show up at the exact moment someone is deciding whether to trust you.

## Gap #5: No path for people who aren't ready to call yet

Not every visitor is ready to pick up the phone the second they land on your site. Some are still just checking if this is even something they need to worry about. A website with only one option — "call now" — loses all of these hesitant visitors completely.

**Fix:** Give people a lower-commitment way to raise their hand. A quick quiz, a savings calculator, or a simple "get a ballpark estimate" tool lets someone who isn't ready to talk yet still leave their contact info.

## Test your own site right now (takes 5 minutes)

Pull up your website on your phone, like a real customer would, and go through this checklist honestly:

- [ ] Can you see your phone number or a "Request a Quote" button without scrolling?
- [ ] Does your main contact form ask for 3 fields or fewer?
- [ ] Does the page load in under 3 seconds? (Test at [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/))
- [ ] Is at least one review or trust badge visible near your main call-to-action?
- [ ] Is there any option on the page besides "call us" — like a quiz or calculator — for visitors who aren't ready to call yet?

If you answered "no" to two or more of these, your site is very likely a brochure site pretending to be a lead-gen site — and that gap is probably costing you real jobs every single week.

## Why fixing this matters more than getting more traffic

It's tempting to think the fix is simply more traffic — more ads, more SEO work, more social media posts. But here's the math that changes everything: if your site converts at 1% instead of 5%, you need **five times** the traffic just to get the same number of leads. Fixing conversion first means every dollar you spend on traffic afterward works five times as hard.
