We talk to a lot of contractors who assume their website problem is a design problem. It usually isn't.

Most sites we review are perfectly fine to look at. Clean layout, decent photos, the logo's in the right spot. And they still generate almost no leads. That's because a website can be visually fine and still fail at the one job it actually has: turning a visitor into a phone call or a form fill before they leave.

## The difference between a brochure and a lead-gen system

A brochure site describes the business. A lead-gen site is built around a single question on every page: what does this visitor do next?

That sounds obvious, but most contractor sites don't answer it. A few of the most common gaps:

- **No clear call to action above the fold.** If a homeowner has to scroll to find your phone number or a "Request a Quote" button, most of them won't bother.
- **Lead forms that ask for too much, too early.** A five-field form with a dropdown for budget range feels like a commitment. A name, phone number, and a one-line description of the problem converts far better.
- **Slow load times on mobile.** The majority of local service searches happen on a phone, often standing in front of the exact problem. A site that takes six seconds to load loses people before they see anything.
- **No proof near the decision point.** Reviews, before/after photos, and licensing/insurance badges work best placed right next to the CTA, not buried on a separate "About" page nobody visits.
- **No path for people who aren't ready to call yet.** Not every visitor wants to pick up the phone immediately. A quick quiz, a savings calculator, or a simple "get a ballpark estimate" tool gives hesitant visitors a lower-commitment way to raise their hand.

## Why this matters more than traffic

It's tempting to think the fix is more traffic — more ads, more SEO, more social posts. But if your site converts at 1% instead of 5%, you need five times the traffic to get the same number of leads. Fixing conversion first means every dollar you spend on traffic afterward works harder.
