"Storm chaser" has a bad reputation for a reason — plenty of outfits show up after a hailstorm, sign homeowners to inflated contracts, and disappear before the job's finished right. But storm-damage roofing is also some of the highest-value, fastest-closing work available, and doing it well (not chasing, just being fast and straight with people) is one of the biggest lead sources a roofing company has.

## Speed is the entire game in the first 48 hours

After a hailstorm or major wind event, homeowners aren't comparison-shopping the way they would for a routine repair — they're trying to figure out if they even have damage, and who to call. The roofer who shows up in their search results or their mailbox within a day or two of the storm gets the inspection appointment. Three days later, that same homeowner has already had someone else's estimate in hand.

That means your response system needs to be ready before the storm hits, not built afterward:

- **A storm-tracking alert** so you know about hail and wind events in your service area as fast as anyone else does.
- **A dedicated storm-damage landing page**, ready to go live same-day, that explains the insurance claim process in plain language.
- **A fast-response ad campaign** you can activate immediately, targeting the specific affected zip codes.

## The insurance claim process is where trust is won or lost

Most homeowners have never filed a roof claim before and don't know what a fair adjustment looks like. This is exactly where a straightforward roofer wins over a pushy one. When an adjuster's initial estimate misses code-required items — ice-and-water shield, proper ventilation, matching shingles on a partial repair — walking the homeowner through a documented supplement request (not a padded one) is routine, not a red flag, and it's the single biggest difference between an honest storm response and the kind that gives the whole category a bad name.

Document everything: drone photos of the full roof, moisture readings, a written scope that matches what the adjuster should have caught. That documentation does double duty — it justifies a fair supplement, and it's the same proof that wins the job over a cheaper, faster-talking competitor in the first place.

## Build the relationships that pay off next storm too

The roofers who do this consistently well end up with something more durable than one good storm season: referral relationships with insurance adjusters who've seen their documentation hold up, and with other trades (especially tree services, who are often on-site at the same storm-damaged properties) who send work back and forth. None of that happens from one aggressive knock-and-close — it happens from being the roofer who did the job right and made the claim process easy to understand.

Storm season isn't a shortcut to volume. It's a test of whether your response speed and your documentation are actually ready before you need them — and the roofers who pass that test every storm are the ones still getting referrals two years later.
